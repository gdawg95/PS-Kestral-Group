### (d) Duplicates, in detail

Two kinds, and they mean different things.

- **18 rows** share a dealership, date, department, rating *and* comment text, in 9 group(s). Identical free text on the same day at the same dealership is almost certainly one piece of feedback entered twice.
- **85 rows**, across 16 dealerships, match on everything but have no comment. Nothing distinguishes them and nothing should: two customers rating Service 5 on the same busy Saturday produce this honestly. Counted, reported, never dropped.
- **63 further rows** came out of the Toowoomba pivot and are identical by construction — a cell saying nine people gave Service a 5 in October becomes nine rows with the same date, score and no comment (D-032 #1). Counting these as coincidences would have put the middle figure at 148 and made Toowoomba look like the worst offender in the pack.

Nothing is de-duplicated automatically. Removing a row changes a dealership's published score, so it needs the owner's decision (D-019).

### What the name row actually means

A raw count against a list of first names is mostly false positives, because
plenty of first names are also ordinary English words. Separating them with the
same test D-026 uses to promote names — a token that appears mostly in lower case
across the corpus is a word, not a name that slipped through:

| | Comments |
|---|---:|
| Matched a name from a name column | 99 |
| …of those, ordinary words that are also someone's name | 99 |
| **…of those, probably a real missed name** | **0** |

So the residual leak is **0 of 5,362 comments (0.00%)**, not 99. This is the residual risk D-026 described: a name used once, in a case the corpus pass could not confirm. The owner's read of samples is the backstop.
